# Methodological Reference

This note translates the methodological orientation of the project references into practical guidance. It is not a substitute for reading and citing the original publications.

## 1. Cultural public sphere and #JogjaOraDidol

The #JogjaOraDidol case can inform an analysis of how cultural expression and public debate intersect in digital spaces. A useful analytical distinction is:

- **Structural:** conditions of access, participation, platform affordances, and visibility.
- **Representational:** how actors, places, conflicts, and public issues are framed through narratives and symbols.
- **Interactional:** how participants respond, contest, amplify, and negotiate meanings.

Possible methods include text exploration, social-network analysis, and discourse analysis. These methods answer different questions and should be integrated rather than treated as interchangeable. A comparative application to another case must establish contextual similarities and differences.

## 2. Principal-agent relations in social media

Principal-agent analysis may help examine representation, delegation, accountability, and information asymmetry in digital communication. Translate the theory into observable indicators instead of assigning roles from popularity metrics.

Questions:
- Who claims to represent whose interests?
- Is there evidence of delegation, authorization, or an accountability relationship?
- What incentives or information asymmetries may shape the relationship?
- What alternative relationship models could explain the same interaction?

A network edge, mention, or repost is not by itself proof of a principal-agent relationship.

## 3. Hybrid methods for revealing facts behind texts

Computational text analysis can identify patterns at scale; qualitative analysis can evaluate meaning, context, ambiguity, and exceptions. A hybrid workflow should document how outputs from one method inform the other.

A practical sequence:
1. Audit and clean the dataset with reversible transformations.
2. Explore terms, patterns, and candidate themes.
3. Select examples using explicit sampling rules.
4. Conduct close reading and qualitative coding.
5. Compare qualitative findings with computational patterns.
6. Revise interpretations and document disagreements.

## 4. Validity and limitations

- Platform data are not equivalent to the population's opinion.
- Engagement metrics are affected by platform design and visibility mechanisms.
- Topic models produce statistical groupings, not inherently valid social categories.
- Network structure depends on the chosen relation and sampling window.
- Text meaning may depend on irony, local references, and conversational context.
- Observational association does not establish causal effects.
