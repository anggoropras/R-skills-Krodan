# Example Analysis Plan: Public Discourse on an Urban Policy

This is a hypothetical plan, not a report of actual findings.

## Research objective

Understand how an urban policy issue is represented and contested in public social-media discussion during a defined observation period.

## Illustrative questions

1. What recurring themes and frames appear in the collected posts?
2. How does discussion volume change around documented policy events?
3. What interaction patterns are visible in reply, mention, or repost relations?
4. How are place, identity, cultural heritage, and public-interest claims represented?
5. What can and cannot be inferred about representation or principal-agent relations?

## Data

A hypothetical CSV containing record IDs, timestamps, text, platform, and optional interaction references. The sampling frame and collection limits must be stated before inference.

## Methods

1. Audit missingness, duplicates, timestamp parsing, and text coverage.
2. Preserve raw text; create a documented normalized-text field.
3. Profile daily or weekly posting volume.
4. Conduct keyword exploration and qualitative coding on a documented sample.
5. Explore themes computationally only if corpus size and quality support it.
6. Build separate networks for each defined interaction relation.
7. Select representative and deviant examples for discourse analysis.
8. Triangulate patterns against policy documents, news, or field sources supplied for the study.

## Interpretation safeguards

- A rise in posts is not automatically a rise in public support or opposition.
- A high-degree node is not necessarily a leader or policy representative.
- Hashtag co-occurrence does not prove coordination.
- A topic label requires contextual validation.
- Causal claims require a separate design and suitable evidence.

## Expected outputs

A data-audit note, method log, findings table, network specification (if applicable), discourse-analysis memo, limitation statement, and policy brief outline.
